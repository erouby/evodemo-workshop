Modelling adaptation with the EvoDemo-Hyper MPM

A hands-on workshop with the evodemohypermpm R package

Author

Etienne Rouby

Published

June 2026

Welcome

This is a hands-on workshop for modelling how populations adapt to selection using the EvoDemo-Hyper matrix population model and its R package, evodemohypermpm.

The method bridges two traditions that have long talked past each other: demographers, who track populations through matrix models, and evolutionary quantitative geneticists, who track heritable traits through the breeder’s equation. The EvoDemo-Hyper MPM does both at once, in a single, fast, transparent matrix — and lets you ask questions like “which kind of species adapts fastest, and does the answer even have a single value?”

By the end you will be able to:

  • run an eco-evolutionary projection for a species of your choice in a single function call;
  • read and visualise its output — population size, evolving trait distributions, and adaptation rate;
  • compare how the same selection pressure plays out across the slow–fast life-history continuum;
  • understand why the answer to “who adapts fastest?” depends on how you define adaptation;
  • (going further) see how the hyperstate matrix is built, reproduce the paper’s theory-vs-simulation comparison, and export your own results to a PDF report.

Who this is for

Anyone comfortable running R code who is curious about evolutionary demography. You do not need to know matrix algebra to get value from the 2-hour core — we run the model first and open the engine later. The “Going further” part is for those who want the machinery.

How this site works

The site is a single reproducible document. Every figure you see was produced by the code printed just above it, so you can copy any chunk straight into your own session.

TipThree kinds of box appear throughout
  • Key idea boxes flag the one thing to remember from a section.
  • Your turn boxes are short exercises. Try them before opening the Solution — predicting the answer first is what makes it stick.
  • Heads-up boxes warn about a modelling pitfall.

You can read it online, or download the whole thing as a PDF from the link in the sidebar.

Schedule (≈ 2 hours + optional depth)

Block Time You will…
1 The big picture 20 min meet the life cycle and the model-in-one-picture
2 Your first model 30 min run MPM(), read its output, make your first plots
3 Selection scenarios 30 min put each vital rate under selection and watch traits evolve
4 The slow–fast continuum 30 min compare five species and confront the “it depends” punchline
★ Under the hood optional build the hyperstate matrix; theory vs simulation
★ Export & extend optional PDF reports, tuning, and extensions
