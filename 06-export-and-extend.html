6  Export & extend

NoteWhat you’ll be able to do

Save a publication-ready figure to PDF, generate a parameterised PDF report of your own run, and point yourself toward the model’s natural extensions.

6.1 Getting things out as PDF

There are two senses of “PDF output”, and the workshop gives you both.

The whole workshop as a PDF. Use the Download PDF link in the sidebar — Quarto renders this entire site to a single document from the same source you have been reading.

A figure as a vector PDF. For your own results, ggsave() writes a clean, scalable PDF you can drop straight into a manuscript. Build the figure once, then save it:

out <- MPM(h2 = 0.2, Vp = 1, Beta = 0.15,
           Fe = species$F[3], SA = species$SA[3], SJ = species$SJ[3],
           S0 = species$SJ[3], Y = species$gamma[3], selectedrate = "SJ")

p <- out$pheno_distribution |>
  filter(year %in% seq(1, 101, by = 25)) |>
  ggplot(aes(phenotypic_midvalue, density, colour = factor(year))) +
  geom_line(linewidth = 0.8) +
  scale_colour_viridis_d(option = "mako", direction = -1, name = "Year") +
  labs(x = "Phenotype value", y = "Density",
       title = "Passerine under juvenile-survival selection")
p
ggsave("my_figure.pdf", p, width = 7, height = 4.2, device = cairo_pdf)

A full parameterised report. The repository ships a template at templates/report.qmd. Render it to PDF for any species and selection scenario without editing the file — just pass parameters:

quarto::quarto_render(
  "templates/report.qmd",
  execute_params = list(species_id = 5, rate = "SA", beta = 0.3, h2 = 0.2)
)

This is the reproducible way to hand a collaborator a tidy PDF for a specific run: the parameters are recorded in the document itself.

6.2 Extending the model

The two-stage life cycle with one trait is a teaching scaffold. The same framework reaches further — here is where to look next, all grounded in the original paper (Van de Walle et al. 2025).

Phenotypic plasticity. In the base model an individual keeps its phenotype for life (the within-life phenotype-transition matrices \(\mathbf{P}_{ij}\) are identities). Make them non-identity and a trait can change with age or with the environment — that is plasticity, expressed in exactly the same matrix language as evolution.

Two-sex inheritance. The model tracks male and female breeding values, so paternal contributions enter the inheritance step explicitly.

Evolving genetic variance. The base model holds \(V_A\), \(V_P\) and \(h^2\) at birth constant. The authors note that letting \(V_A\) vary is computationally cheap to add, though it requires committing to assumptions about genetic architecture.

Richer life cycles. Nothing restricts the demography to two stages. The vec-permutation construction scales to any stage-structured life cycle — including the kinds of long-lived marine and terrestrial vertebrates these methods were built for.

6.3 Capstone — your turn

ImportantA question to take home

Pick a species you actually study (or the closest of our five). Decide which vital rate selection most plausibly acts on, and which definition of adaptation rate answers your real question — per year for a management horizon, per generation for a genetic one. Run it, plot the trait distribution through time, and export a one-figure PDF. You now have a reproducible eco-evolutionary projection for your system — and a clear-eyed sense of what its adaptation rate does and does not mean.

6.4 Where the code lives

  • Package (install from here): the evodemohypermpm repository on the CNRS Koda forge.
  • Full code (MATLAB + R + the individual-based model): fledge-whoi/Eco-EvoHyperstateModel on GitHub.
  • Reproduction archive for the paper’s figures: the project’s Zenodo deposit.
  • The paper: Van de Walle, Garnier, Bonnet & Jenouvrier (2025), Methods in Ecology and Evolution (Van de Walle et al. 2025) — the citation for any work using the model.

Thank you for working through it. Go model an adaptation rate, and report the number rather than guessing it.

Van de Walle, Joanie, Jimmy Garnier, Timothée Bonnet, and Stephanie Jenouvrier. 2025. “Toward a Unified Approach to Modelling Adaptation Among Demographers and Evolutionary Ecologists.” Methods in Ecology and Evolution 16 (8): 1644–57. https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.70075.
